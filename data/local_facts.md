# Verified local water facts: Columbus & Central Ohio

Compiled 2026-10-01. Every fact below comes from a primary source (utility Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) / utility web page / Ohio EPA / Ohio Department of Health) and lists its URL.
Third-party aggregators (waterhardness.org, tapwaterdata, EWG, etc.) were **not** used as fact sources.
"Data year" is the calendar year the CCR covers. Most CCRs here are the **2025 reports (published 2026)**. Before publishing, re-check each figure against the newest CCR every year (utilities publish by July 1).

Units: 17.1 mg/L (ppm) as CaCO3 = 1 grain per gallon (gpg).

---

## 1. City of Columbus (Columbus Water & Power, Division of Water)

**Source:** 2025 Consumer Confidence (Water Quality) Report, data year 2025:
https://www.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/8/services/public-utilities/water-reports/consumer-confidence-water-quality-report/consumer-confidence-report.pdf

- **Water sources:** "surface water from the Scioto River and Big Walnut Creek" plus groundwater "from the sand and gravel deposits of the Scioto River Valley" pumped at the Parsons Avenue plant.
- **Three treatment plants:**
  - **Dublin Road Water Plant (DRWP):** Scioto River via Griggs & O'Shaughnessy reservoirs. Serves the northwest and southwest of the system.
  - **Hap Cremean Water Plant (HCWP):** Hoover Reservoir on Big Walnut Creek. Serves OSU area and the north.
  - **Parsons Avenue Water Plant (PAWP):** wells (groundwater). Serves the southeast.
- **Treatment:** the multi-barrier process includes lime + soda ash/caustic softening ("to remove calcium and magnesium ions that are responsible for water hardness"), filtration, and disinfection. UV disinfection is also used at the surface-water plants.
- **Hardness, 2025 annual averages (ranges):**

  | Plant | mg/L | gpg |
  |---|---|---|
  | Dublin Road | 125 (122-132) | 7.3 (7.1-7.7) |
  | Hap Cremean | 98 (85-118) | 5.7 (5.0-6.9) |
  | Parsons Ave | 123 (122-127) | 7.2 (7.1-7.4) |
  | System summary table, hardness as CaCO3 | 115 | |

- **Nitrate, 2025 (MCL 10 ppm):**
  - DRWP: up to 6.3 ppm (range ND-6.3)
  - HCWP: 1.4 ppm (ND-1.4)
  - PAWP: ND
  - The CCR says the Scioto River "can experience elevated levels of nitrate due to agricultural" runoff. Columbus added an **anion-exchange nitrate removal process at the Dublin Road plant**.
- **Sodium, 2025 averages:** DRWP 69.2 ppm (37.6-119.7), HCWP 19.2 ppm, PAWP 86.7 ppm (77.8-102.2). Sources listed: "naturally occurring; treatment process; road salt."
- **Sulfate, 2025 averages (SMCL 250):** DRWP 129.3 ppm, HCWP 54.5 ppm, PAWP 207.8 ppm (176.3-233.1).
- **Chloride:** DRWP 62 ppm, HCWP 31 ppm, PAWP 62 ppm. **Total alkalinity:** 63 / 39 / 43 ppm.
- **Disinfection byproducts (2025, plant averages, all in compliance):**
  - TTHM: 58.4 / 64.1 / 27.6 ppb (MCL 80)
  - HAA5: 22.4 / 37 / 4.7 ppb (MCL 60)
- **Agricultural herbicides:** atrazine detected at DRWP 0.2 ppb avg (max 0.84) and HCWP 0.11 ppb (MCL 3).
- **Cryptosporidium:** "In 2025, Crypto was detected 2 out of 12 times" in Scioto source-water samples (raw water, before treatment).
- **Lead:**
  - 90th percentile 1.3 ppb (2023 sampling; action level 15). 0 of 50 sites exceeded.
  - Water leaving the plants is below detection.
  - Columbus offers **free drinking-water lead testing**.
  - In 2024 the city established a **Lead Service Line Replacement Program to remove all public and private lead and galvanized service lines by 2040** (columbus.gov/LSLR), plus a LEAP no-interest loan for early replacement.
- **PFAS:** the CCR notes the 2024 U.S. EPA PFAS MCLs. Utilities "will be required to begin monitoring in 2027 and comply" by 2029, as written at the time of the CCR. See section 9 for the 2026 federal changes.

**Hardness page:** https://www.columbus.gov/Services/Columbus-Water-Power/About-Columbus-Water-Power/The-Division-of-Water/Water-Facts/Water-Hardness
- Columbus "softens its water on average to 120 ppm, approximately 7 grains per gallon," which it classes as **moderately hard**.

**PFAS page:** https://www.columbus.gov/Services/Columbus-Water-Power/About-Columbus-Water-Power/The-Division-of-Water/Water-Resources-for-Customers/PFAS-and-Drinking-Water
- Columbus has tested for PFAS since 2019.
- Some PFAS have been detected at very low levels near detection limits.
- UCMR5 federal monitoring began February 2024.
- The city states its three plants would currently comply with the federal standards.

**Filter guidance (CCR FAQ):** asked whether customers need a home filter, Columbus answers no. Customers who choose one should use a filter **certified to NSF/ANSI or WQA standards**.
> Content implication: pages must not claim Columbus water is unsafe. Position filtration and softening as taste, odor, scale, comfort and preference upgrades. The verifiable hooks are moderate hardness of ~7 gpg, chlorine/disinfection taste, sodium and sulfate at PAWP, and seasonal nitrate at DRWP.

**Communities on Columbus water:** https://www.columbus.gov/Services/Columbus-Water-Power/About-Columbus-Water-Power/The-Division-of-Water-Reclamation/Wastewater-Treatment/Water-Sewer-Contract-Communities
- Contract communities served with water and/or sewer include Bexley, Dublin, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Grove City, Groveport, Hilliard, Lockbourne, Marble Cliff, Minerva Park, New Albany, Obetz, Reynoldsburg, Riverlea, Shawnee Hills, Upper Arlington, Urbancrest, Valleyview, Whitehall, Worthington, parts of Westerville, Brice, Perry Twp, Jefferson Twp JWSD, various Delaware County unincorporated areas, and others.
- The page says the city **does not** provide service to Canal Winchester, Pickerington, parts of Westerville, and some townships, e.g. **Blendon Township (Aqua Ohio)**.
- Caveat: the list covers "water and/or sewer." Confirm water specifically before stating it on a page. The CCR service-area map also shows these suburbs.

## 2. Westerville
https://www.westerville.org/services/water
- Westerville operates its **own 7.5 MGD water plant** with lime and caustic soda softening.
- Water is "softened to approximately 125 mg/l or 7 grains per gallon."
- Columbus also lists "various parts" of Westerville as contract areas (above).

## 3. City of Delaware
https://www.delawareohio.net/government/departments/public-utilities/our-utilities
- Own water plant. Primary source is the **Olentangy River**, which can be blended with groundwater from wells more than 200 ft deep.
- 3.65 MGD capacity, more than 12,000 customers.
- Hardness: **not verified**. The city CCR is an image-based eReport, so leave it blank.

## 4. Del-Co Water Company (Powell, Sunbury, much of Delaware County and beyond)
- **Hardness** (https://delcowater.org/water-hardness/): "approximately 7-8 grains per gallon (120-140 mg/L)."
- **2025 CCR** (https://delcowater.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-Web-Ready-CCR.pdf): sources are the Olentangy River, O'Shaughnessy Reservoir (Scioto River), Alum Creek Reservoir, and a well field in Knox County. Nearly 13 MGD delivered.
- **Powell** is served by Del-Co: https://www.cityofpowell.us/Residents/Public-Service-Requests/Utilities
- **Sunbury** is served by Del-Co; the city bills sewer only, based on Del-Co usage data: https://sunburyohio.org/192/Utilities
- Lewis Center and Orange Twp are not stated here. Del-Co asks property owners to request a water availability letter, so service is property-specific. Left blank in locations.csv.

## 5. Newark
2025 CCR: http://www.newarkohio.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025-CCR.pdf
- Source: **North Fork of the Licking River** (surface water). Lime softening.
- Hardness **119 mg/L (~7 gpg)**. Nitrate up to 6.02 ppm (MCL 10).

## 6. Heath
- 2025 CCR: https://www.heathohio.gov/s/2025CCR-z3kr.pdf
- Utilities page: https://www.heathohio.gov/utilities
- Source: groundwater from **four well fields** (Hoback Park on Dorsey Mill Rd, and Irving Wick Dr). The buried-valley sand-and-gravel aquifer lies 150-200 ft deep and parallels the South Fork Licking River. Low susceptibility.
- Treatment: aeration, alum + lime softening, recarbonation, sand filtration.
- CCR quote: "The city water is softened to **145 mg/l** making home water softeners unnecessary."
> Content implication: do **not** push softener sales copy on a Heath page. Use filtration, RO and taste angles instead, or skip softener pages for Heath. This is already reflected in page_plan.md.

## 7. Granville (village)
- 2025 CCR: https://www.granville.oh.us/s/CCR-2025.pdf
- Utilities page: https://www.granville.oh.us/utilities
- Source: groundwater from **three wells** in a village-owned well field near Raccoon Creek, next to the treatment plant.
- The aquifer "has a high susceptibility to contamination." An Ohio EPA Superfund site near the well field has been monitored for 28+ years.
- No hardness figure is published in the CCR.

## 8. Johnstown
2025 CCR: https://static.showit.com/file/DRFJUoIH2zbpLrxaJjPG4g/273542/johnstown_2025_ccr_updated.pdf
- Own plant at 395 W. Jersey St. Water comes from **three groundwater wells** at Belt Park.
- The aquifer is more than 130 ft deep under about 130 ft of clay, so Ohio EPA rates it **low susceptibility**.
- The plant checks hardness daily, but no hardness figure is published.

## 9. Ohio EPA: PFAS (statewide context)
https://epa.ohio.gov/monitor-pollution/pollution-issues/per-and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas
- **2020 statewide sampling:** 1,550 public water systems tested. 94.5% had no PFAS detections, 80 had detections below action levels, and 2 were above.
- **Circleville** received a **$2,937,000** grant to design a 2.0 MGD reverse-osmosis membrane treatment plant.
- **Columbus** received **$2,999,857** for planning granular activated carbon (GAC) improvements at the Dublin Road plant, plus **$538,130** for LC/MS/MS PFAS lab equipment.
- **Private wells:** the Ohio Department of Health plans up to 600 PFAS samples from private wells statewide.
- **Federal status in 2026:** U.S. EPA's May 2026 proposals would extend PFOA/PFOS compliance to April 2031 and rescind the standards for the other PFAS. Ohio paused its own PFAS rulemaking.
> Content implication: there's no evidence of Columbus PFAS exceedances. PFAS pages should be educational: what certified RO and GAC remove, plus links to these sources. No fear claims.

## 10. Circleville
https://circlevilleoh.gov/public_utilities/wtp/
- Source: **four wells about 130 ft deep in the Teays aquifer**. Treatment is iron/manganese filtration only, with no softening.
- City quote: "Our water is considered hard (**25 grains/gallon**)." That's about 428 mg/L, the hardest verified figure in the area.
- Planned RO plant: see the Ohio EPA PFAS grant above.
> Content implication: Circleville is the strongest verified **softener and RO** market in the dataset.

## 16. Private wells (Ohio Department of Health)
- **Program page:** https://odh.ohio.gov/know-our-programs/private-water-systems-program
  - A "private water system" serves fewer than 15 service connections and fewer than 25 people.
- **Water quality & treatment page:** https://odh.ohio.gov/know-our-programs/private-water-systems-program/water-quality-treatment/quality
  - Permits for new or altered private systems require **total coliform, E. coli and nitrate** sampling.
  - ODH recommends regular testing of private wells for coliform, E. coli, nitrate and arsenic.
  - The secondary (aesthetic) standard for **iron is 0.3 mg/L**.
  - **Manganese greensand "iron filters"** are common, for iron up to about 10-15 mg/L.
  - The nitrate MCL is 10 mg/L.
  - The page links USGS work on **arsenic in Licking County groundwater**.
> Content implication: this is the basis for the county well-water and well-testing pages. Cite ODH. Don't claim a specific township's wells contain anything without data.
